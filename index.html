A second pair of eyes, that speaks.

SecondEye recognises faces from a camera feed and tells you who is there by voice. It is built for people who can't easily see faces, and for anyone who wants a quiet assistant when names slip away.

Interactive demo

Try it with your own face.

Register your face with your webcam, then watch SecondEye recognise you and say your name. It follows the logic of recognize.py: compare each face to stored embeddings, accept it under a distance threshold, confirm it over two checks, then announce it. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded. No camera? Switch to the simulated scene.

How it works

Four stages, one script each.

The repository keeps each stage in its own file, so every step can be run and tested alone.

01

Register

Capture a person's face and store it under their name.

register.py
02

Embed

Builds the embedding database that recognition loads at start-up.

create_embeddings.py
03

Recognise

Detects faces with OpenCV, embeds them with Facenet512 through DeepFace, and matches by cosine distance.

recognize.py
04

Run the app

Tie recognition and spoken output together in one entry point.

app.py
Engineering

Built to be tested, not just demoed.

Each behaviour that matters has a test script in the repo.

Test scripts

  • test_database.py checks stored identities
  • test_recognition.py checks matching
  • test_multiple_faces.py checks crowded frames
  • test_voice.py checks spoken output

Design goals

  • Stable announcements: a person must be seen on two consecutive checks before it speaks
  • Non-blocking voice: offline pyttsx3 runs on a background queue, so the camera loop never waits
  • Tunable: distance threshold 0.30, recognition every 10 frames
  • Honest scope: it identifies the largest face today, and multi-face identification is next
